Doily Décor — Bringing Vintage Lace Back Into the Modern Home

Pinterest's 2026 trend report says it plainly: "the doily makes its big debut in 2026." Here's how to use crochet lace as a year-round decorating tool — not just something that comes out for the holidays.

By HomeEssentials · September 2026 · 6 min read

For most of the last two decades, the doily has been treated as something to inherit and store, not something to buy. That's changing fast. Pinterest's most recent trend report singled out lace as one of the defining materials of 2026, with searches for lace doilies, lace nails, and lace-adjacent home decor all climbing sharply — part of a broader return to handwork, texture, and things that look like someone made them.

For a Grandma Core home, this is close to a perfect trend match. Doilies and crochet lace already sit at the center of the aesthetic — they just haven't been given their own spotlight the way tablecloths and ceramics have. Here's how to use lace as a decorating layer that works in any room, any season.

Where doilies actually work in a modern home

Under a lamp, a candle, or a small vase

The simplest possible use, and still the most effective: a single crochet doily underneath a lamp base or a votive candle instantly adds texture to a surface that would otherwise be bare wood or glass. Mix sizes — a small doily under a candle, a slightly larger one under a lamp on the same shelf.

Layered under a table runner

Instead of a full lace tablecloth (which can feel overtly "special occasion"), let a few inches of a round doily peek out from under a linen runner at each end of the table. It reads as collected-over-time rather than costume-y.

As a wall or window treatment

Lace curtains in a kitchen or reading nook window filter light beautifully and add pattern without needing any other decoration on that wall. This is one of the lowest-effort, highest-impact doily-adjacent moves in the whole aesthetic.

Grouped as a garland or wall hanging

Several doilies of different sizes, sewn or pinned in a loose vertical line, make a simple and genuinely striking wall hanging — no framing, no hardware beyond a single nail.

🧵 The mixing rule: Doilies work best in odd-numbered clusters of varying sizes rather than a single uniform doily alone on a surface. Three different sizes stacked or overlapped slightly looks intentional; one doily by itself can look accidental.

Doily décor beyond the table

Because the trend is about lace as a material, not just the classic round crochet doily, it's worth thinking beyond tabletops. A lace-trimmed pillow cover, doilies stitched onto the hem of a curtain, or a single large crochet piece used as a small wall tapestry all draw on the same visual language without needing a formal table setting at all.

Doily Décor — where to start

  • A set of assorted-size crochet doilies for lamps, candles and shelves
  • Lace curtains for a kitchen or reading nook window
  • A doily layered under a table runner rather than replacing it entirely
  • A small cluster of doilies as an easy, low-cost wall hanging
  • Mix sizes and let the edges show — a doily that's fully hidden defeats the point
